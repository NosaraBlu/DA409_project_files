Why Police Data Matters

Police data is public because it shapes daily life in a community.

Virginia agencies collect and publish crime and enforcement data through state and federal reporting systems. This site organizes public sources in one place so readers can compare patterns across five jurisdictions.

Each section summarizes reported trends and links back to original sources for verification. The goal is clarity and context, not commentary.

Data sources include the Virginia State Police, local department reports, the FBI’s National Incident-Based Reporting System (NIBRS), and the Virginia Open Data Portal.

Five Areas Under the Lens

Not the same Virginia.

Northern Virginia jurisdictions tend to report lower violent crime rates than larger independent cities. Differences in density, mobility, and local economies shape what is reported and how it trends over time.

Five Jurisdictions

Arlington County

Group A offenses increased in 2024 compared to 2023. Preliminary 2025 data suggests stabilization.

City of Alexandria

Violent crime decreased slightly while property crime showed modest increases.

Fairfax County

One of the largest jurisdictions in Virginia, with consistently low violent crime rates.

City of Richmond

Homicides have declined since 2022, though overall violent crime remains above state average.

City of Norfolk

Part of the Hampton Roads region, reporting higher violent crime rates relative to Northern Virginia.

Data Sources and Methodology

All data is traceable to public sources.

Crime statistics follow the FBI’s NIBRS structure as collected through Virginia reporting pipelines. Population figures are provisional estimates from the Weldon Cooper Center at the University of Virginia.
